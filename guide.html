First-Timer's Guide

What to expect when you ask for help

Walking into a food pantry or calling a crisis line for the first time is scary. This page describes what usually happens, what to bring, and what to ask — so you know what to expect before you go.

This guide gives general information about accessing resources in Terre Haute. Programs, eligibility rules, required documents, hours, funding, and availability all change. Call before traveling and ask the organization what applies to your situation.

Getting identification when documents are missing

There is no single order that works for everyone. Each office has its own document rules. Call before traveling and ask exactly what will be accepted in your situation.

1

Contact Vigo County Vital Statistics about your birth certificate

Certified copies of Indiana birth certificates for births recorded in Vigo County are issued here. Identification and eligibility requirements apply, and fees and accepted payment methods can change.

Office: Vigo County Vital Statistics, 171 Oak St, Terre Haute, IN 47807
Phone: 812-462-3442
In-person applications: Mon–Fri 8:15am–3:15pm. Arrive before 3:15pm to apply for a birth certificate that day.
Office hours: The office may stay open after 3:15pm for other business. Call to confirm its hours and holiday closures.
Before you go: Call and ask which identification documents and payment methods are accepted.
2

Contact Social Security about replacing your card

A replacement Social Security card is free. The Social Security Administration requires acceptable evidence of identity and, where applicable, citizenship or lawful status.

National line: 1-800-772-1213
Local office: 222 Cherry St, Terre Haute, IN 47807
Official information: SSA replacement card page
Cost: Free
3

Review Indiana BMV identification card requirements

An Indiana identification card normally costs $9. A free ID card may be issued if you are at least 18, are a United States citizen, and are eligible to vote.

The BMV requires documents proving identity, lawful status, Social Security number, and two documents proving Indiana residency. Check the official list before traveling.

Terre Haute BMV: 2920 Professional Ln, Terre Haute, IN 47802
Phone: 888-692-6841
Standard fee: $9
Official requirements: Indiana identification card page

If you are not sure where to start, call 211. Indiana 211 may be able to identify local case management, document recovery, or transportation resources. Financial assistance is not guaranteed and depends on current program funding.

What to expect at each service

First visits are intimidating. Here is what actually happens when you walk in the door.

Food

Walking into a food pantry

Catholic Charities: 812-232-1447 — 430 N 14 1/2 St  |  Salvation Army: 812-232-4081 — 234 S 8th St

What to bring Requirements vary by pantry and by distribution event. Some request identification or proof of address; others are walk-up events. Call before traveling to confirm what applies that day.
What happens The process depends on the pantry. You may be asked about household size, residency, or eligibility, or the event may run as a walk-up distribution. Waiting time depends on attendance and available food.
How long Wait times vary by location, event, and attendance.
Common fear

They will ask a million questions and turn me away or make me feel ashamed.

The reality

Pantry staff and volunteers are there to help. Giving out food is what a pantry is for, and you are allowed to be there.

Housing

Looking for emergency lodging

Light House Mission: 812-232-7001 — 1201 S 13th St  |  Current availability: Call 211

Before going Call first whenever you can. Available space, who is served, identification requirements, arrival times, and house rules all change.
What happens Staff will ask about your immediate situation and explain current availability and intake requirements. Calling 211 can also help identify other options that night.
Important A listing does not guarantee an available bed. If you are fleeing domestic abuse or sexual assault, call CODA Wabash Valley at 800-566-2632 for 24/7 mobile advocacy.
The reality

Shelter availability changes quickly. Calling before you travel gives you the best chance of getting accurate instructions for that day.

Crisis

Calling 988 or a crisis line for the first time

988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline  |  Hamilton Center crisis line: 800-742-0787

What to bring Nothing. Just your phone. You can call or text 988, and it is free.
What happens When you call 988 you hear a short greeting with menu options, such as the Veterans Crisis Line and Spanish-language services. Hold music plays while you are connected to a trained crisis counselor, who introduces themselves, asks about your safety, and listens. Hamilton Center's line is answered 24 hours a day for crisis assessments and referrals.
Is it confidential 988 says your conversation is confidential, and you do not have to give your name or other personal information to get support. Confidential is not the same as anonymous: the counselor can see your phone number, and what you share may be written down. 988's policies allow some information to be shared in certain situations, such as to coordinate your care, when the law requires it, or with your agreement. If someone is in immediate danger, emergency services may be contacted. You can ask the counselor not to share your personal information. Read 988's confidentiality page and privacy policy.
Common fear

They will send the police to my door and have me committed against my will.

The reality

Most calls are a conversation. The federal agency that oversees 988 says most crises are handled by counselors without involving law enforcement. A small percentage of calls lead to 911 being contacted, when there is an imminent risk to someone's life that cannot be reduced during the call. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 911.

Healthcare

Getting medical care without insurance

Wabash Valley Health Center: 812-232-7447 — 1436 Locust St  |  Hamilton Center Grace Clinic: 812-231-8377 — 622 8th Ave

What to bring Call first to make an appointment and ask what to bring. Both clinics use a sliding fee scale, so ask what proof of income is needed.
What happens Staff will ask about your insurance and income to work out your fee. Wabash Valley Health Center has navigators who help people sign up for health coverage, and Grace Clinic says it can help you find an insurance plan.
Cost Fees depend on your income and the care you need. Ask about the sliding fee scale when you call. Grace Clinic states that no one will be turned away for inability to pay.
Common fear

They will turn me away or hand me a bill I cannot pay.

The reality

Both clinics offer reduced fees for patients who qualify. Ask about the cost before your visit so there are no surprises.

Legal

Applying to Indiana Legal Services for the first time

Indiana Legal Services: 1-844-243-8570 — phone applications Mon–Thu 10am–2pm Eastern  |  Apply online

What to bring Be ready to describe your legal problem and your household income and assets. The online application takes about 20 minutes.
What happens You apply by phone or online. Staff review your application and decide whether they can take the case. Indiana Legal Services says you should hear back within about 14 days. Applying does not guarantee that your case will be accepted. There is no Terre Haute office; the Bloomington office serves Vigo County.
Who qualifies People with low income and limited assets who have a civil (non-criminal) legal problem, such as an eviction, a family law matter, a benefits denial, or a consumer issue. Income is generally within 125% of the federal poverty guidelines, and up to 200% in some circumstances. Some projects use different rules. If you are unsure, apply.
Common fear

My problem is not serious enough or I will sound stupid and they will say no.

The reality

They handle everyday civil problems like evictions, benefit denials, and custody. Explain your situation honestly. If they cannot take your case, ask where else you can turn.

Mental Health

First appointment at Hamilton Center

Hamilton Center: 812-231-8323 — 620 8th Ave

What to bring ID and an insurance card if you have them. If you do not have insurance, ask about fees when you call.
What happens Call first. Staff will ask some basic questions and schedule you. At a first appointment, expect to talk about what is going on now and what kind of help you want. Ask how long to plan for.
Cost Costs may be covered by insurance, Medicaid, or Medicare. Hamilton Center publishes a sliding fee scale, and Indiana residents may qualify for fee adjustments based on family size, income, and diagnosis. Ask about payment plans.
Common fear

They will think I am crazy or put me on medication I do not want.

The reality

A first appointment is mostly about understanding your situation and what kind of help you are looking for. You can ask questions about any treatment that is suggested.

Housing

Applying for Section 8 through the Terre Haute Housing Authority

Terre Haute Housing Authority: 812-232-1381 — 2001 N 19th St

What to bring Birth certificates and Social Security cards for each person in the household, photo ID for adults, and income verification.
What happens Call first to ask whether the waiting list is open. Applications are picked up at the office and returned by mail or in person; incomplete applications are not accepted. If you are placed on the list, the Housing Authority contacts you when your name comes up.
Honest reality The waiting list is not always open, and the wait can be long. Ask how long the current wait is, and keep your contact information updated with the Housing Authority so they can reach you when your turn comes.
Common fear

I will never get approved or the wait is too long to bother.

The reality

It can be slow, but it is real help when it comes through. Pursue other housing options at the same time. Do not wait on Section 8 alone.

Recovery

Walking into sober living intake

Odyssey House: 812-231-8600 — 1547 S 7th St  |  Hamilton Center Oak Street House: 800-742-0787

What to bring Ask when you call. Houses usually expect personal clothing and hygiene items, and ID if you have it. No drugs or alcohol.
What happens Call first. Ask about current openings, costs, house rules, and who the house serves. Odyssey House and Oak Street House are both state-certified recovery residences. Oak Street House is run by Hamilton Center; ask about its eligibility requirements.
The rules Rules vary by house. Common ones include no drugs or alcohol, meeting attendance, chores, fees or rent, and drug testing. Ask for the rules before you move in.
Common fear

They will judge me for my past or kick me out the moment I struggle.

The reality

Recovery residences expect you to be serious about recovery, and they are used to people arriving nervous. You will not be the first person who walked in scared.

About this site

Terre Haute Help is a free, independent resource directory. Information is researched using official websites, public information, and direct contact with providers when possible. Programs, hours, eligibility, and availability can change, so confirm details with the provider before traveling and report outdated information to us. This site has no ads, no paywalls, and no affiliation with any government agency.

To report an incorrect or outdated listing, email terrehautehelp@gmail.com.
